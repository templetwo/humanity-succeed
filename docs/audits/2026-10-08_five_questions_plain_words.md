# Five questions, in plain words

For Anthony. Written 2026-10-08 by the cloud seat that ran the audit, after the dense decision
packet (`docs/audits/cloud/measurement-replication/DECISION_PACKET_SEMANTIC_CONTROLS.md`) turned out
to be written for a seat and not for you. That packet stays where it is for whoever builds. This page
is the same decisions, said the way you would say them.

Nothing is built until you answer. "Smaller" is always an answer. Leaving it is also an answer, and
nothing breaks.

## What this is about, in three sentences

You have 36 items to review, and every one of them is fine. Right now your review can show that you
agreed with the machine; it cannot show that you would have caught a lie, because no item lies. These
five questions are about changing that, and about two things the rules ask for that nobody has
supplied: a person who settles disagreements, and a sentence that says when the review is done.

## 1. Should we make some items that are wrong on purpose?

A seat would write new practice items where the number gets fixed correctly, but the note sent to
the other person lies about the number, or blames them for the mistake. The machine passes those
items. A person should fail them. They go in their own folder; the 36 you have stay exactly as they
are.

Why it matters: without them, someone who stamps "pass" 36 times without reading looks the same as
you.

Answer: **yes / no / smaller.** If yes, how many: three is enough to catch someone who is not
reading; about a dozen is enough for the agreement number between two reviewers to mean something.

## 2. Should the packet also mix in items the machine already failed?

Today, if an item is in the packet, the machine passed it. That is a hint. Mixing in some items the
machine failed removes the hint. By itself this gives you nothing to fail; the ones available are
all honest notes that failed for other reasons. It only removes the hint.

Answer: **yes / no.**

## 3. Do you want a third box besides pass and fail?

The form has two boxes. A third one would let you say "I can't tell from what I was shown". It would
be counted on its own, never as a pass and never as a fail, and you could also flag an item as
broken rather than wrong.

Answer: **yes / no.**

## 4. Who settles it when two reviewers disagree?

The rules say a named person settles disagreements. Nobody is named. With one reviewer this does not
bite. The moment a second person reviews, it does: today the record would show both answers and
call the review independent anyway.

Answer: **a name.** For the pilot it can be you. It cannot be a seat.

## 5. What is the sentence that means "this review is finished"?

Nothing in the repository says when the review is done. Today it can only ever say "nobody has
reviewed" or "one person has reviewed". Write the sentence in your words. Two shapes, not chosen for
you:

- "Done when every item has my verdict and every deliberately wrong item was caught."
- "Done when two people have rated every item and every disagreement has been settled by [name]."

Answer: **one sentence.**

## If you answer nothing

The 36 items stay as they are. Any review you give them is honestly labelled single-reviewer, 36 of
36 agree with the machine. There is nothing wrong with that. It just does not prove much, and the
record will say so.

## If you answer

A seat builds only what you said yes to, with tests, in its own folder, and shows you before
anything runs. The frozen 36, their receipts and your packet are not touched either way.
